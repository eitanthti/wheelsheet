The held rate is a frequency count, not a profit backtest.

It answers one narrow question. Over the last two years, how often was this stock still above the strike a 30-day put would have used? It says nothing about what a seller would have earned, and this page is about everything it leaves out.

The exact rule

What the number counts

The generator walks the last 500 trading days. For each day it recomputes the strike as it would have been set that day, then looks once at the close 21 trading days later.

Step 1 · the strike of that day

Take the stored close and the stored 30-day implied volatility for day t. Solve for the strike whose put delta is about 0.30 at 30 days. That strike moves every day, because both the price and the IV move.

Step 2 · one look, 21 days later

Compare the close on day t plus 21 trading days to that strike. One comparison per day. Above the strike counts as held. At or below counts as not held.

Step 3 · the share

The held rate is the share of days that counted as held. The sheet also carries held_n, the number of windows that actually had both a price and an IV. The median this week is 481 and the lowest is 358.

Worked example · STX

Spot 805.55 and IV 64.4% put the 0.30-delta 30-day strike at 746.01 for a model premium of 31.31. Its held rate is 78.9% over 484 windows. Read that as: in about 382 of 484 past windows the close 21 trading days on was above the strike computed that day.

Read this before you use it

Seven things the held rate does not do

Each one can move a real outcome in the direction that costs money. None of them is modelled.

It is not a P&L

A held window earns the premium. A not-held window is assigned and loses whatever the gap below the strike is worth. A 78% held rate with deep losses in the other 22% can still lose money over the same two years.

It ignores the path

Only the close 21 trading days later is checked. A stock can trade far below the strike for three weeks, recover on the last day, and count as held. American puts can be exercised early, which that count never sees.

No fills, no spreads, no fees

The premium is a Black-Scholes number from the day's IV. No bid, ask, slippage or commission enters it. Real fills on a wide chain are worse, and the sheet republishes no quote to check that with.

No dividends and no corporate actions

Closes are used as stored. Ex-dividend drops and splits are not adjusted for in the strike walk. Both shift how often a close sits above a strike.

The windows overlap

500 daily windows over two years are not 500 independent trials. They share most of their days. The true number of independent observations is nearer 24, so a 78% rate has a much wider error bar than 484 samples would suggest.

It is one regime

Two years is the whole sample. It contains whatever the last two years happened to be. A stock that only rose has a high held rate for that reason alone.

Survivorship, quietly

Today's universe is walked backwards. Names that were delisted, acquired or collapsed are not in it. That biases every held rate in the same optimistic direction.

The IV used is the IV of that day

Where the stored IV history is stale, the strike for that day is wrong, and so is the window. The issue table now excludes any name whose IV is more than 5 days old, but the CSV keeps them with an iv_age_days column.

Why the default is 70

The floor is a filter, not a finding

The sheet drops names under a 70% held rate by default. That number is a convention, not a result.

70% is roughly the median of the scored universe, so the floor keeps the upper half. This week it passes 61% of the 149 names scored. It is not a level anyone showed to be profitable, and it is the first filter worth changing.

A high held rate is not a free lunch either. Across the 12 names in this week's table, annualised yield and held rate move together, with a correlation of +0.53. The names that stayed above their strike most often are also being paid well, which usually means the market has a reason to pay them.

Every one of these numbers is in the CSV attached to each issue. held_rate_pct and held_n are columns. Recompute them your own way and the sheet will not argue.
